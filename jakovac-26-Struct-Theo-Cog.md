A Structural Theory of Cognitive Representation and Problem Solving
  Contexts, Invariance, and the Knowledge Space
Antal Jakovac 1,2 and András Telcs 1,3
May 6, 2026

# Abstract

* Learning and problem solving depend critically on 
  the structure of internal reprs. While 
* modern systems achieve strong predictive performance, 
* hE their reprs often lack explicit structure for expressing 
  abstraction, invariance, and task-relevant regularities
* We: a minimal structural framework for organizing reprs in which 
  * basic cognitive primitives become explicit. 
  * context := a partition of a subset of the world state space,
    * The central notion
    * fixes the distinctions, granularity, and admissible form of problem
      formulations
* Within this setting, 
  invariance recognition and representative selection are introduced as
  fundamental repral operations. 
* The framework is realized as a Knowledge Space composed of 
  two coupled graph structures: 
  a Concept Graph that hosts constructed and refined concepts and 
  a Procedure Graph that encodes typed operations over reprs.
  * Together, these structures provide 
    a minimal cognitive algebra for operating on reprs 
    without assuming sophisticated inference, learning, or control mechanisms.
* simple illustrative examples, 
  * we show that appropriate repral organization can 
    substantially simplify the form and scope of admissible regularities, 
    even when problem solving is carried out by a fixed and weak solver. 
* The contribution of the paper is structural rather than algorithmic: it
  * minimal repral prerequisites for abstraction and invariance, and provides 
  * a foundation on which richer learning, evaluation, and control mechanisms
    can be developed in subsequent work.
